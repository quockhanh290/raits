What each panel means

Every panel below answers three questions in the same order: what it is for, where its numbers come from, and when you should not believe it. The third one is the reason this page exists — most numbers here are honest about being absent and quiet about being stale.

Words used everywhere

Five words appear on nearly every panel. They are worth reading once.

sleeve
One trading strategy with its own instrument and its own hours. There are four, and their hours never overlap. All times below are New York time, which is the only clock this dashboard uses. NKD trades a Japanese index while the US is asleep, 01:10–02:55. Calm decides at 09:32 and reads its price at 10:02. Stress runs 10:35–12:30, Swing 14:05–15:55.
slot
One scheduled moment when a sleeve looks at the market and decides. Slots repeat every five minutes inside the sleeve's hours — 71 of them across the whole day, about twenty per sleeve. Each one leaves a record whether or not it found anything.
regime
One word for the kind of market we are in — Calm, Normal, or Stress. It is worked out once a day from the S&P 500. Each sleeve is only allowed to trade in certain regimes, so this word decides whether a sleeve even looks.
shadow
The system is running for real but cannot place an order. It watches, decides and writes everything down; the last step is switched off deliberately while the record is being built up. Anything the dashboard calls a decision is a decision that was not acted on.
gate
A condition that must be true before an order could ever be sent. Gates are listed on the Runtime panel. While any gate is closed, orders are impossible no matter what the strategies decide.

Now Monitor

What this answers

Is anything wrong right now that a person should act on. When nothing is, this panel is empty and says CLEAR — that is the normal state, not a panel that failed to load.

Where the numbers come from

It is assembled in the browser from several live readings: whether the broker connection dropped, whether the positions the broker reports match the ones the system thinks it holds, whether a scheduled job is unresolved, and whether the price input the strategies depend on has gone stale. Each item carries its own problem, impact and suggested action.

When not to trust it

It only knows about things that report themselves. A job that never started raises nothing here — its absence shows up on the Job journal instead. Treat an empty Now Monitor as "nothing has complained", not as "everything has been checked".

Open Issues

What this answers

What is still unresolved, going back weeks. Unlike Now Monitor, this is a standing ledger: an item stays until there is positive evidence it was fixed.

Where the numbers come from

Reconstructed from the scheduler's own logs — the record of every job that ran, failed, or was missed. Nothing is written by hand.

2 open / 3 retired legacy
Two items still count against the system. Three belong to the older trading route that has been switched off; they are kept for history and collapsed by default.
evidence 2026-07-30 to 2026-09-05
The span of logs this was built from. Anything outside that window is not missing — it was never looked at.

When not to trust it

An item disappearing does not always mean it was fixed. It can also mean the log it was reconstructed from has aged out of the window above. Check the span before reading a shorter list as progress.

Track 1 Runtime

What this answers

Could this system place an order right now, and if not, what is stopping it. This is the panel that says how close the project is to trading with real money.

Where the numbers come from

Read from the files the system writes about itself as it runs — one per day, kept permanently.

Orders possible: no
The plain answer. Below it are the specific conditions holding it shut.
Window coverage — 10 day(s), latest … 2/4 sleeves complete
How many days have a full, vouched-for record, and how many sleeves finished cleanly on the most recent one. A sleeve that has not reached its hours yet is not a failure.
Slot timing — p95 168.3s over 71 record(s)
How long a slot takes from start to finish. "p95" means 95 out of 100 finished faster than this; it is a way of describing the slow ones without letting a single outlier set the number. Slots are five minutes apart, so anything approaching 300s is worth attention.
Paper account baseline
The account value the system compares against. It has a shelf life, and the panel says so when it has passed it.

When not to trust it

This panel is the slowest read on the page — on a fresh load it can sit saying "reading" for several seconds. That is not a failure. What matters is whether the gates listed here match what you expect; a gate quietly opening is a bigger event than any number on this page.

Track 1 Market View

What this answers

For one sleeve, on one session: what did each of its rules see, slot by slot, and why did it or did it not produce a trade. This is where you go to answer "why did nothing happen today".

Where the numbers come from

The session bar (09-05, 09-04, …)
Which trading day the panel is describing. Days are listed only if something was actually recorded for them — a dashed one has slot outcomes but no detailed diagnostics, so its conditions are re-derived from stored prices rather than read from what the slot wrote. Only this band follows the day you pick; everything else on the page stays on today.
Progress and outcome chips
Two separate facts, deliberately kept apart. The first says where the session is — WAITING before the sleeve's hours, LIVE during, COMPLETE after. The second says what it found. "No signal" on a live sleeve is an interim reading; on a complete one it is the session's answer.
Rule lanes — one cell per slot
Each row is one rule, each cell one slot: passed, failed, or never reached because an earlier rule stopped first. "3/22 pass" counts slots, not bars.
Conditions and Readings
Conditions carry a verdict. Readings are measurements the detector took but did not judge — they are shown because they explain the verdict, not because they are one.
RECORDED / RECONSTRUCTED
The most important label on the panel. RECORDED means the slot wrote this down while it ran. RECONSTRUCTED means the slot wrote nothing and the same logic was replayed afterwards over stored prices — real, reproducible, but not evidence that the live route did it.
The candle chart
Prices from the stored history, which is refreshed once a day in the early afternoon. Before that refresh it shows the previous session, and says so on its own chip. The line chart beneath it is a different thing: one point per slot, written as each slot ran, so it shows today while the candles still show yesterday. Both are labelled with their date.

When not to trust it

Check the two dates before comparing the charts to each other — they are routinely a day apart and both are correct. And a RECONSTRUCTED card is not proof the route ran: it is proof the rules, replayed now, would have said this.

Calm · two phases

What this answers

This sleeve decides in two steps rather than one, so it gets its own panel. At 09:32 it decides whether a setup exists. At 10:02 it reads the price that setup would trade at. Both steps are shown because a setup with no price is a normal, expected state, not half a result.

Where the numbers come from

From what each phase recorded at the time. This sleeve can act on several instruments at once, so when more than one is involved each gets its own block with its own numbers — an earlier version showed only the last one.

When not to trust it

A stop price shown against the 09:32 phase would be invented: at half past nine the entry price it is measured from does not exist yet. If you ever see one there, something is wrong with the panel, not with the strategy.

Regime Monitor

What this answers

Markets behave differently at different times — some stretches are quiet, some choppy, some violent. This panel says which kind of stretch the system currently believes we are in, and how sure it is. It matters because each sleeve is only allowed to trade in certain stretches.

Where the numbers come from

Every trading day the system takes the S&P 500 ETF's closing prices and measures two things: how much it moved that day, and how jumpy the last five days have been. A statistical model turns those two numbers into one word — Calm, Normal, or Stress. The word is worked out once a day by a scheduled job and written down; this panel reads what was written, it does not work it out itself.

Confidence 91.1%
How strongly the model favours the word it chose. Above roughly 95% is not a close call.
Runner-up Normal 8.9%
The next most likely word and how much support it has. When this climbs, the picture is becoming mixed.
Lead 82.1 pp
The gap between those two, in percentage points.
Uncertainty — Entropy 0.435 of a possible 1.585
One number for how spread out the model's opinion is. Zero means completely sure. The maximum, 1.585, means it cannot tell the three possibilities apart at all. So 0.435 is about a quarter of the way to having no opinion.
Held 18 days
How long the current word has been unchanged.
Model fitted through Dec 31, 2024 · 21 mo stale
The model's settings were frozen on that date and have not been re-learned since. This is deliberate: a model that re-learns itself moves the goalposts every time it is measured. The age is shown so the choice stays visible, not because it is a fault.
Shift threshold — none published
There is no cut-off number to cross. The model picks whichever word fits best, so a change happens when a different word takes the lead, not when a level is breached.

When not to trust it

The word is written down by a job, not read live. If that job does not run, this panel keeps showing yesterday's word and nothing here looks wrong — the date beside the label is the only clue. Check that it is the last trading day. If it is behind, every sleeve gated on the regime is deciding on an old answer.

Open Positions

What this answers

What the broker says is actually held, next to what the older trading route's own records say. The two are shown together because a disagreement between them is the thing worth seeing.

Where the numbers come from

The left side is the broker, read live. The right side is the older route's saved state. The panel is labelled "legacy / drain" because that route is being wound down — it is not expected to open anything new.

When not to trust it

"No broker positions" and "the broker could not be reached" are different facts. If the broker is unavailable the panel says so; an empty table with no warning means genuinely nothing is held.

Today's Decision

What this answers

What the older trading route did on its most recent active day: how many trades it considered, took, and closed.

Where the numbers come from

The last snapshot that route wrote. That route is switched off, so this snapshot stops advancing — when it is old enough, the counters are hidden and replaced by a line saying so, because zeros from a retired route read exactly like a quiet day.

When not to trust it

If you are looking for what happened today, this is the wrong panel — use Track 1 Market View. This one describes a route that is no longer trading.

Open Orders

What this answers

Which orders are sitting at the broker right now, waiting. In practice these are protective stop orders attached to open positions.

Where the numbers come from

Read live from the broker each time the page polls.

When not to trust it

While the system is in shadow mode it cannot place orders, so an empty table is the expected state and says nothing about whether the strategies found anything.

Job Journal

What this answers

Every scheduled task that ran today, in order, with what it did. This is where you look when a panel is empty and you want to know whether the work behind it happened at all.

Where the numbers come from

The scheduler's own record, one row per execution. Clicking a row opens what that run left behind — the decision it reached, and the evidence for it.

When not to trust it

A task that was never scheduled leaves no row, and no row looks the same as a quiet day. The Runtime panel's coverage count is the check for that: it says how many slots were expected, not just how many appeared.

Source Clocks

What this answers

How old each piece of information on this page is. Every other panel shows values; this one shows their age.

Where the numbers come from

Runner observed
When the older route last wrote a snapshot. On a machine running only the new route this is deliberately old, and the row says "legacy route, retired" when it is.
Schedule freshness
Whether another scheduled slot is still due today. It is not a statement about the route's health — it can read "fresh" beside an observation from last week, and both are correct.
Expected next
When the next scheduled task is due.
Broker observed
When the broker was last read successfully.
Browser poll
How often this page re-asks for everything.

When not to trust it

All times here are in US market time, not your local clock, and not the machine's. Times on this page never mean anything else.
